Workshop · chart page & chart details dialog · round 2

Comments that point at a spot in the chart

Round 2, rebuilt around four redlines. A comment points at one second of the chart, the conversation lives in a sticky panel at the bottom of the strip on every host, and you start one by double-clicking (or double-tapping) the spot itself.

An anchor is an attribute of a comment, not a new kind of comment. A comment gains one optional number — AnchorAt, chart seconds, the same clock the rail's pins use. Everything shipped keeps working unchanged: the audience rail, personal notes, votes, replies, moderation, translation, the 500-character plain-text body, the purge. The step chart is a lens over the comments that carry a time; the Comments tab shows every comment and marks the anchored ones with a chip that jumps to the strip.
Redline 1 · scrollbar overlapThe marks lane on narrow layouts now sits 10 px clear of the strip's edge, out from under the phone's overlay scrollbar; the desktop column moved inboard too.
Redline 2 · times onlySections and ranges are gone: no stripes, no map drag, no anchor menu. The chip is the time. Two comments on the same second stack.
Redline 3 · one panel, every hostThe desktop column is gone. Page, phone and dialog all carry the same sticky panel at the bottom of the strip; Open thread goes to the Comments tab.
Redline 4 · double-click to commentNo toolbar button, no two-step. Double-click or double-tap a spot and the panel becomes the composer for that second.

The vocabulary

four marks, one chip, one panel
SpotA bubble on the annotation rail at the comment's second, with a tick into the strip so the eye lands on the arrows it means.
Stack ×NComments on the same second share a bubble with a count — the pin rule, reused. The panel pages through them.
Your noteGold and hollow, like your runs on the rail. Only you see it, whatever scope you are reading.
SelectedThe bubble turns primary and a hairline crosses the strip at its second: "this row, this comment".
ComposingAfter a double-click: a hollow + bubble and a primary hairline mark the second you are writing about, until you post or cancel.
On the mapEvery comment is a dot on the whole-chart map's left edge, so the overview shows where the talk is — deaths keep the right edge.

The panel

  • It follows the strip. It always shows the comment nearest to what you are looking at, with ‹ › to walk the chart's comments in order — each step scrolls the strip along. Tapping a mark does the same thing as walking to it.
  • It is the reader's whole loop for one comment: time chip, author, two lines (tap to unfold), ▲ vote, reply count, and Open thread ›, which switches to the Comments tab with the comment focused.
  • It is also the composer. Double-click the strip and the panel turns into the line, chip first: 0:38, Posting to Public as ERRLENA, Cancel, Comment. The picked second stays marked on the strip while you type.
  • Empty state teaches the gesture: "No comments on this chart yet — double-click a spot to add one." On charts that have comments, the ＋ in the panel's corner carries the same hint on hover and works as the accessible way in.

The chip is the link everywhere else: on a comment row in the Comments tab it switches to Steps, scrolls the strip to the second and selects the mark.

Reading

  • The column is gone. The panel at the bottom of the strip is the whole reading surface, on desktop exactly as on a phone — one component, three hosts.
  • Scroll, and it keeps up. Whatever comment is nearest the middle of the viewport is the one in the panel; ‹ › jump to the previous and next and scroll the strip there. Hover a mark for the same card as a tooltip; click it to land on it.
  • Which comments? The scope is a quiet text menu on the legend row — Comments: Public ▾ — the Sort / Read-in shape. Your notes overlay whatever scope is chosen (open question 2).
  • Open thread › on the page opens the chart details dialog on its Comments tab, focused on that comment — the way /WeeklyCharts opens dialogs from a static page (open question 1).

Writing

  • Double-click an arrow row. The pick snaps to the nearest arrow row within a beat's reach (two people who double-click the same quad stack on the same second), otherwise the exact second. Hovering the strip still shows the hairline with the second, so you know where a double-click would land.
  • The panel becomes the composer with the chip already filled; ✕ on the chip or Cancel drops the pick. Post, and the new bubble appears on the rail with the panel on it.
  • No spot, no post: the composer only ever exists for a second — general comments keep their home in the Comments tab.

Reading on a phone

  • Narrow layout, one addition: the pins already moved into the time gutter and the rail's width went back to the arrows, so the marks get a slim lane past the strip's right edge — and now that lane stops 10 px short of the box edge, where the overlay scrollbar draws. Nothing sits under the thumb.

Writing on a phone

  • Double-tap the spot. The strip's scroll box takes touch-action: manipulation, which keeps pan and pinch and removes double-tap zoom, so a double-tap can mean "comment here" without the browser zooming first.
  • The panel turns into the composer; the keyboard rises under it and the picked second stays marked above. The chip's ✕ backs out.
  • No toolbar button: the gesture is the affordance, the empty panel names it, and the ＋ in the panel corner is the accessible fallback.

Steps tab

  • Compact strip, compact rail: marks take a third column past the death pins, inboard of the edge. The panel is the same one, 380 px up the box.
  • Open in Comments › switches tabs with the comment focused — the FocusCommentId path the moderation queue already uses. Arriving from the strip lands you in the thread with your place kept.
  • Double-click works here as everywhere; the post lands in the Comments tab with its chip.
  • The legend gains Comments · 7 and Your notes · 1 (open question 4).

Comments tab

  • Every comment, as today. An anchored one wears its time chip under the name; tapping the chip switches to Steps, scrolls the strip to the second and selects the mark. Unanchored comments look exactly as they do now.
  • The composer here stays the plain line — a comment about the whole chart. To write about a second, you go to the strip and double-click it; that is the only door, and it is the obvious one.
  • A reply inherits its root's second — a thread is about the spot. The author can move the second when editing; it is an edit like any other (revision row, "(edited)").

Open questions

also listed in the chat message — this is the durable copy
  1. Where "Open thread" goes on the page. The page has no comments today. Mocked: it opens the chart details dialog on the Comments tab, focused — the /WeeklyCharts pattern, zero new surfaces. The alternative is a Comments section on the page. Recommendation: the dialog now; a page section is its own slice.
  2. Your notes overlay every scope on the strip, gold — or Notes stays a scope you switch to, as in the dialog today. Recommendation: overlay; your runs already behave this way on the rail.
  3. Hosts. Both hosts in one slice, or the dialog first (it has the thread already) and the page second? Recommendation: both — the panel is one component and the module is one file.
  4. Legend counts ("Comments · 7", "Your notes · 1") in the Steps tab. The Comments tab was denied a count badge because it cost a query at nineteen call sites; here the count rides a fetch the tab already makes. Fine, or keep the tab silent?
  5. Snap to the nearest arrow row on double-click (within ~0.12 s), so two comments about the same quad share a bubble — or take the exact second and let near-misses sit as separate bubbles? Recommendation: snap.

Settled by the redlines: times only, no sections or ranges; one sticky panel on every host; double-click / double-tap places; marks on the right, inboard of the scrollbar. The span cards' rail fact went with the spans.
