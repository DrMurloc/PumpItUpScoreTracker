Phoenix 2 · scoring analysis

Two rulers for the same shelf

Phoenix 2's PUMBILITY and our Competitive Level both turn a chart and a score into one number, and both are used to say who is better. Across the doubles 18–23 band they mostly agree — within half a level everywhere above A+. Then, on barely-passed charts, on singles, and on players with thin score histories, they come apart completely. Every chart below re-reads against the controls.

01 · the shape of each ruler

What one chart is worth, on a scale both formulas can read

Every play is priced as the level of chart a clean SSS+ would have to be to be worth the same, then plotted as credit lost against the chart's own level — so 0 means "worth exactly its level" and −3 means "worth three levels less". One panel per chart level. Competitive Level is a straight line; PUMBILITY is a staircase, because it reads the letter grade, not the score.

06 · the source

The two formulas, as implemented

Ported verbatim from the repository and checked against the 30 golden per-chart rows in Phoenix2PumbilityScoringTests — every one reproduces to the cent.

Phoenix 2 PUMBILITY

Base(L) = 130 + 5·L + 5·max(0, L−24)
singles price at Base(L+1)

chart = Base × (gradeMult + plateBonus)

total   = Σ top 50 across both types
singles = Σ top 50 singles
doubles = Σ top 50 doubles
  • Grade multipliers run 1.28 (A) → 1.50 (SSS+) — an 17% spread over the whole grade ladder.
  • Plate is an additive term, RG 0.000 → PG 0.020.
  • Charts below level 10 and broken plays price at zero; CO-OP never counts.
  • ScoringConfiguration.Phoenix2PumbilityScoring

Competitive Level

chart = L + (score − 965,000) / 17,500
singles ≥ L20 ×= 1.008^(L−19)

combined = avg of top 100
singles  = avg of top 50
doubles  = avg of top 50
  • Linear and continuous: 17,500 points of score = one level, at every level and every grade.
  • Plate is ignored entirely; broken plays are excluded from the pool.
  • No floor — a D23 at 700,000 prices at 7.86.
  • ScoringConfiguration.CalculateFungScore, aggregated in PlayerRatingSaga

How the shared ruler is built

The two numbers are not comparable as they stand — one is a few hundred points per chart, the other is a level. Both are restated as the doubles level whose clean SSS+ / Marvelous play is worth the same. On the PUMBILITY side that means dividing the chart's value by the anchor performance (1.50 + 0.006) to recover an implied Base, then inverting the base curve — piecewise, since Base grows 10 per level above 24. On the Competitive side it means subtracting the same anchor's score term ((995,000 − 965,000) / 17,500 = 1.714). Both sides therefore return the chart's own level for that one play, and every other cell is a real difference.

This is a normalisation invented for this page, not a formula the site ships. It is per chart — a player's PUMBILITY total is deliberately never mapped to a level, because sum-of-50 against average-of-100 has no honest single conversion (that is the whole of panel 05). The site's nearest relative, SuggestedTitleLevel, does the same trick at a different granularity: it inverts a title's 50-chart threshold to a folder at a fixed AAA / Talented reference.

The anchor choice moves the small numbers and not the large one. Re-anchoring on AAA / Talented gives a worst above-A+ disagreement of 0.55 instead of 0.46; on S / Marvelous, 0.37; on A+ / Rough, 0.84. Read "under a level above A+" as the finding, not the second decimal. The A-column fork barely moves at all across those same anchors — +4.06 to +4.77 — which is why that one is quoted as a result.

07 · what falls out of it

Findings

On doubles, they are very nearly the same ruler above A+

This was the surprise. Over the whole D18–D23 × A+→SSS+ grid the two metrics never disagree by more than half a level (0.46 on this page's anchor; under a level on any anchor — see panel 06), and usually by under 0.2. The reason is arithmetic coincidence rather than design: PUMBILITY's base curve grows 5 points per level on a base of 220–245, so one level is worth ~2.1% of a chart's value, and the grade ladder happens to spend about that much per 17,000–20,000 points of score in the S range. That is Competitive Level's 17,500 to within rounding.

The fork is the A band, and it is a structural one

PUMBILITY reads the grade, so the A band — 100,000 points wide — is worth exactly the same play at 800,000 as at 899,999. Competitive Level moves 5.71 levels across that same span. That single band produces the whole +4 level disagreement, and it is why the two metrics rank grinding and pushing so differently: to PUMBILITY a barely-passed D23 is worth 95% of a polished D18, and to Competitive Level it is worth six levels less.

Singles is where the per-chart formulas actually disagree

Both formulas pay a singles bonus, and they pay it in different shapes. PUMBILITY prices a single one level up its base curve — a flat +2.1% at every level in this band. Competitive Level multiplies by 1.008^(L−19), which pays nothing below level 20 and then compounds: +0.17 of a level at 20, +0.79 at 23. Flip the chart-type control and the worst above-A+ disagreement widens from 0.46 to 1.3 levels — three times the doubles figure, and all of it from the bonus, not the core curve.

Sum versus average is the bigger divergence

The per-chart formulas mostly agree; the aggregations do not. PUMBILITY sums 50 charts, so it is a volume metric — a player with 15 excellent scores can only ever reach 30% of the number. Competitive Level averages, so those same 15 scores produce the highest competitive level on the board. The "Sniper" archetype in panel 05 is exactly this: best competitive level of the four, worst PUMBILITY by a factor of three.

Two implementation notes worth a look

  • Singles Competitive Level selects and scores by different numbers. In PlayerRatingSaga.RecalculateCore the per-type pools order by the type-aware Fung score (which applies the 1.008^(L−19) singles multiplier) and then average the type-blind one. The selected 50 is therefore not the top 50 by the value being averaged — at high level the multiplier reorders enough to swap charts near the cutoff. Doubles is unaffected; the multiplier is singles-only.
  • The sub-A grade multipliers are still extrapolated. B and below are extended at the last observed −0.05 step and marked TODO(P2-pumbility). That region does not matter for D18–D23 comparisons above A, but every number this page shows left of the A floor inherits that uncertainty.
